Fourteen years ago, I was actively using heroin and felt like I was in an endless trap. I used to binge-watch the TV show Lockup and fantasize that if I were in prison, I could probably stop shooting dope.

I was desperate, and I was searching the internet constantly for ways out of addiction. I didn't know anyone in recovery, and I didn't know anything about the 12 steps. I honestly thought the only way I could end up in treatment was if the TV show Intervention showed up at my house.

Back then, I stumbled onto an article about a psychedelic drug called ibogaine. It seemed to have a lot of promise, but I didn't have the resources or the knowledge to get to a foreign country that used it as therapy for heroin addicts. I remember reading the stories and being very curious.

Now the federal government is paying for the groundwork to study it here.

## What was announced

Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. [announced on November 12](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-advances-ibogaine-ptsd-research-rapid-acting-mental-health-treatments.html), the day after Veterans Day, that the department's research agency ARPA-H will commit up to $4.9 million over five years. The announcement included a statement from Veterans Affairs Secretary Doug Collins.

Kennedy has been pushing ibogaine for a while. At a Senate hearing he [called it](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/rfk-jr-praises-ibogaine-for-depression-treatment-is-the-psychedelic-a-magic-bullet) the most promising treatment for depression and PTSD "that anybody's ever seen." In April, President Trump signed an executive order to speed up research on ibogaine and other psychedelics.

He also comes at this as a man in recovery. Kennedy [has said](https://www.aol.com/news/rfk-jr-says-overcoming-heroin-204743292.html) he was addicted to heroin for 14 years, starting in his early teens, and that his recovery began after an arrest in 1983 (the year I was born!). He says he has been in recovery for 43 years and still goes to 12-step meetings every day. He has credited a book by Carl Jung with turning him back toward faith and into those meetings.

The first awards are for post-traumatic stress disorder, including in veterans. They are not for addiction. But HHS said more awards are coming that will test ibogaine for substance use disorders, opioid use disorder among them.

Two teams were chosen. Mark Cuban Cost Plus Drug Company will work on the regulatory and manufacturing steps for a U.S.-made ibogaine product. Beond will supply data on safety, dosing and how long effects last. Beond runs an ibogaine clinic in Cancún, Mexico.

## What ibogaine is said to do

Ibogaine comes from the root of a West African shrub. Heroin users in the 1960s were [the first to say](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10556383/) that one dose stopped their withdrawal and quieted the cravings afterward, sometimes for months.

People still say the same thing today. Many also describe a long, dreamlike experience in which they look back over their lives.

Most of what is known involves heroin and other opioids. [One clinic study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5996271) reported fewer cravings for both heroin and cocaine, and animal research points to effects on alcohol and nicotine use too. How it works is not settled. [Researchers who reviewed the studies](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10556383/) describe a drug that acts on many brain systems at once.

The evidence so far comes mostly from small studies without comparison groups. No large controlled trial has shown that ibogaine works.

## What the funding does not do

HHS said the awards do not pay for patient treatment, do not authorize clinical trials and are not FDA approval. Ibogaine remains a Schedule I drug under federal law.

The department also warned that ibogaine carries serious risks, including heart rhythm problems that can be fatal, and said any future study will need cardiac monitoring and independent safety oversight.

## An old idea in recovery

The thought that a "psychic change" can break an addiction is older than the 12 steps.

Around 1930, the Swiss psychiatrist Carl Jung told an American patient, Rowland Hazard, that medicine had nothing left to offer him. Jung said the only cases like his that he had seen recover had gone through a deep spiritual experience. Hazard went looking for one, got sober, and passed the idea along a chain of people that reached Bill W., who co-founded Alcoholics Anonymous.

Decades later, Jung wrote to Bill W. that the craving for alcohol was a low-level form of "the spiritual thirst of our being for wholeness." He summed up the remedy in Latin: spiritus contra spiritum. Spirit against spirits.

Bill W. took the idea a step further. In the 1950s he took LSD under medical supervision, hoping it might open that kind of experience to alcoholics who could not get there any other way. In a letter quoted in AA's biography of him, *Pass It On*, he called LSD "of some value to some people, and practically no damage to anyone." He also wrote that it would never replace the work of recovery.

## What comes next

Alongside the ibogaine money, the National Institute of Mental Health opened a $20 million funding call for a research network on fast-acting treatments such as ketamine and psilocybin. Recipients will be named in summer 2027.
